# The Em-Dash / Colon / Semicolon Tax

> Em dashes, colons, and semicolons are the fingerprint of AI-generated prose when overused. Ration them hard. Most become periods.

## Why

Models reach for the em dash, the colon, and the semicolon constantly — they
splice loosely related clauses into one grammatically tidy sentence instead of
committing to two plain ones. A human writer uses these marks a few times per
piece. AI uses them a few times per paragraph. The result reads fluent but
synthetic: every sentence has the same braided shape, and the reader clocks the
pattern even if they can't name it.

The tell is not any single dash. It's the *density*. Three em dashes in a
paragraph, a colon opening every section, a semicolon where a period belonged —
that accumulation is what screams "generated."

## The rule

Ration each mark. Rough budget for a normal piece:

- **Em dash** — at most one per few paragraphs. It's for a genuine
  interruption or a sharp aside, not a comma you felt was too weak.
- **Colon** — reserve for introducing a real list or a deliberate reveal. Not
  for gluing two statements that a period would separate cleanly.
- **Semicolon** — nearly always replace with a period. In conversational prose
  it almost never earns its keep.

Default to the period. Two short sentences beat one spliced sentence.

## Fixes

| Overused | Fixed |
|----------|-------|
| Invitations expire after 7 days — you can revoke a pending invite anytime. | Invitations expire after 7 days. You can revoke a pending invite anytime. |
| Three updates landed this week: invitations, password reset, and logs. | Three updates landed this week. Invitations, password reset, and a full notification log. |
| The link expires after one hour; every active session is signed out. | The link expires after one hour. Every other active session signs out. |
| When Orbit texts a merchant — a bounce notice, for example — you see it. | When Orbit texts a merchant, say a bounce notice, you see exactly what went out. |

Notice each fix trades the mark for a period, a comma, or a plain restart. The
meaning survives. The seam disappears.

## Pattern

On the final pass, count the marks. If a paragraph has more than one em dash,
one colon, or any semicolon, treat each as guilty until proven necessary:

1. Try replacing it with a period. Read both aloud.
2. If the period version reads clean, keep it. It usually does.
3. Only keep the mark when it's doing work a period can't — a true aside, a
   real list intro, a beat you actually want mid-sentence.

## When the mark earns it

- **One em dash** to land a genuine aside or reversal still hits hard *because*
  it's rare. Rarity is the whole value.
- **A colon before an actual list** or a deliberate one-word reveal.
- **A semicolon** only in formal or academic prose where the tight clause link
  is intentional and the register supports it.

Default is the period. Keep the fancy mark when it's earning, and never let two
share a paragraph.
